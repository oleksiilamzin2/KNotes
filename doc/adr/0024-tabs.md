# 0024. Tabs in the sidebar

Status: accepted, 2026-11-13

## Context

Knotes shows one document at a time, with a back/forward history. The owner asked for tabs: a
third segment beside "Теки | Теги" over the sidebar, listing the documents open, and answered
a question round (2026-11-13).

## Decision

- The sidebar's switch has a third segment, "Вкладки": the sidebar lists the tabs, one row per
  document (a note or a PDF), the one shown highlighted. A click on a row shows its document;
  a cross on the row closes it. The note list keeps what it showed.
- Every document opened becomes a tab, or brings back its tab when it has one (owner's choice
  over opening in the current tab, with Cmd+click for a new one). A new tab goes last. Closing
  the tab shown shows the next one (the one before it when it was last); closing the last tab
  leaves the note column empty.
- A tab remembers where its document was: for a note, the paragraph at the top and the
  selection; for a PDF, the place at the top. Coming back to it shows that.
- The back/forward history stays one for the window, as before (owner's choice), not one per
  tab.
- Tabs and their places are kept between launches, per vault, in Knotes's data for the vault
  (beside its index, not in the vault: nothing to sync or commit).
- A document renamed or moved keeps its tab; a deleted one loses it.
- Cmd+W still closes the window.

## Consequences

- Tabs pile up as documents are opened; they are closed by hand.

## Built (2026-11-13)

- The sidebar switch's third segment (`square.on.square`) lists the tabs in the sidebar's
  outline view, a document icon and the name as the toolbar shows it (no `.md` or `.pdf`), a
  cross at the row's end; the tab shown is the selected row. The choice is `sidebar: tabs` in
  `settings-v2.json`, as the other two.
- Every way of opening a document goes through the window's opening of a note or a PDF, so
  the list, links, search results, the history and new notes all make or bring back tabs.
- A tab's place is taken as its document is left: a note's paragraph at the top (its UTF-16
  offset, as the outline finds it) and its selection; a PDF's page shown and how far down it
  the top of the view is. Coming back scrolls there before a search's words are shown (they
  win), and a link naming a PDF page wins over the PDF's place. A tab closed gives its place
  to no other: only the document of the tab shown is measured.
- `tabs.json` beside the vault's index holds the tabs, their places and the tab shown; it is
  written as documents are left (on every switch, on closing a window, on quitting) and when
  a tab closes, and read on the vault's first snapshot, the tab shown then opened again.
  Tabs of documents gone from the vault are dropped then and on every snapshot; moves rename
  them as they rename the history.
